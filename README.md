# Poetry
A poem about code, survival, and the ghost we commit to version control.

This piece explores the tension between human creativity and the relentless machinery of software development. It is a reflection on the grind, the voices, and what we bury to keep moving forward.

## THE BOY WHO WRITES

> I buried a boy beneath my keyboard,
> but every night he returns smelling of moonlight.
>
> I was a boy who wanted to write sonnets about the moon
> before my hands started bleeding logic.
>
> Then hunger came
> and taught me the syntax of survival.
>
> Every compilation error
> reminded me of the bags of bones beneath my bed.
>
> I learned to call them bugs
> so I would not have to call them ghosts.
>
> To debug these dreams,
> I commit murders to my repository.
>
> Each commit buries another version of the boy I was.
>
> Every function I called
> gave my demons new mouths to argue through.
>
> They grew louder each time
> I mistook a stack trace for a prophecy.
>
> When my computer goes dark,
> I send my body abroad
> while my soul stays behind,
> guarding the life I never chose.
>
> By morning,
> I compile the boy
> back into the machine.

*Daramola Femi &copy*
